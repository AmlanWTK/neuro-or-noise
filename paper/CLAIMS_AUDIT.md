### D1 in detail — the error

| Check | 40 epochs | 25 epochs | |
|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | FAIL | FAIL | same (budget-independent) |
| V2 | FLAG (0.091) | **FAIL (0.114)** | **flips** |
| V3 | FAIL (p=1.00) | FAIL (p=0.625) | same |
| V4 | FAIL | **?** | no comparable run — the 25-epoch number used a *hand-picked* 30–45 Hz band, not the uniform-third protocol |
| V5 | PASS | **?** | no comparable run — P3 reported epoch-level accuracy, V5 uses subject-level |

**What is true:** the overall verdict (NOT WELL-POSED) is unchanged, and V1/V3
are unchanged. **What is not true:** that all five verdicts are unchanged.

**Fix, two options.**

1. *Cheap and honest (no compute).* Rewrite as: "Reducing the budget to 25
   epochs leaves the overall verdict and the V1/V3 outcomes unchanged; the V2
   quantity rises from 0.091 to 0.114, crossing our nominal threshold, which
   illustrates why we recommend reporting the quantity rather than the label.
   V4 and V5 were not rerun at the reduced budget."
2. *Better (~1.5 h).* Actually run BandCheck at `--max-epochs 25` and report a
   real, apples-to-apples sensitivity table.

Option 2 is worth it. It is one unattended run and it turns a caveat into a
table — and a genuine sensitivity analysis is exactly the kind of thing that
distinguishes a protocol paper from a checklist.

## E2 closed — "the convention is widespread" (13 Aug)

**Verdict: VERIFIED, but not by the evidence we planned to use.**

The plan was to cite 3–5 individual EEG-MDD papers that evaluate at epoch level.
That plan was abandoned, and the reason matters more than the result.

### Why the original plan was dropped

Every candidate is paywalled. Across two independent search passes covering
~11 papers, **the evaluation protocol could not be confirmed from primary text
for a single one of them.** ScienceDirect blocks automated access, IEEE Xplore
returns nothing, PMC serves a CAPTCHA, Springer exposes only abstracts — and
abstracts do not describe split rules.

What was available was second-hand: review tables and a third-party audit saying
what those papers did. Naming individual papers as leaky on that basis would
have been precisely the error this audit exists to prevent — a load-bearing
claim with no primary source, about *other people's work*, in a submitted
manuscript. Not done.

### What replaced it — stronger, and verified

| Ref | Evidence | Verified from |
|---|---|---|
| Brookshire et al. 2024, *Front. Neurosci.* 18:1373515 | Survey of **63** deep-learning EEG papers since 2018 (depression among the six conditions searched): **61.9% (39)** segment-based holdout, **27.0% (17)** subject-based, **4.8% (3)** both, **6.3% (4)** unclear. Reproducing published architectures under a subject-wise split: Alzheimer's **99.8% → 53.0%**, seizure **79.1% → 65.1%**. | Frontiers full text **and** the medRxiv preprint, independently — figures agree exactly |
| Saeb et al. 2017, *GigaScience* 6(5):gix019 | Across 62 studies, median error **5.60%** record-wise vs **13.00%** subject-wise, p<0.01. | OUP article page |
| Kapoor & Narayanan 2023, *Patterns* 4(9):100804 | Leakage documented in **294 papers across 17 fields**. | NSF-PAR full text; BibTeX from the authors' own site |

This is better than the original plan in three ways: it is a *quantified* claim
rather than an existence proof; it comes from a peer-reviewed audit rather than
from our own reading of other people's methods sections; and it lets the paper
make the fairness argument **without accusing any individual paper**, which was
always the risk in this framing.

### Negative findings worth keeping

These cost search time and correct three of my own wrong priors:

- **Three papers I assumed were Mumtaz-dataset examples are not.** Uyulan et al.
  2021 uses a 46/46 Turkish cohort; DepHNN (Sharma et al. 2021) uses 21/24 with
  a Neuroscan device; Thoduparambil et al. 2020 uses PRED+CT (New Mexico).
- **The convention is not universal on *this* dataset.** Ke et al. 2021 (*Front.
  Comput. Neurosci.* 15:773147) partitions Mumtaz by subject — 24/6 HC and 27/7
  MDD, disjoint — and still reports 99±0.08%. MS-MDDNet (2026) and Bagherzadeh
  et al. (2025) also state subject-disjoint evaluation on Mumtaz. The draft
  therefore says "the field's default", not "universal".
- **Ke et al. 2021 — resolved, and now cited as [9].** See below.

**Two corrections to my first draft of this claim**, both from the independent
check, both now applied to the manuscript:

1. I wrote "under a subject-disjoint partition" as though Ke et al. asserted it.
   They do not — it is *our* inference from their Table 2. Now: "from counts
   consistent with a subject-disjoint partition."
2. I wrote that the ±0.08% "therefore does not reflect between-subject
   variability." The paper never says what it ranges over, so the "therefore"
   was mine. Now the structural argument carries it: a fixed, non-rotated
   13-subject test set cannot express between-subject variability *whatever* the
   ±0.08% ranges over.

**Why this is worth the page space.** It answers the strongest reviewer
objection available against this paper — *others report 99% on this dataset with
a proper subject split, so your 0.847 is just a weak reimplementation.* It is
not a leakage accusation: their split appears clean. It is about the reporting
unit, which is a distinct point and one the paper is already built around.

**Deliberately not claimed:** that epoch-level reporting *explains* their 99%.
It does not follow — they may include task recordings (18,442 × 4 s ÷ 64 ≈ 19
min/subject exceeds the ~10 min of EC+EO rest, but the paper does not say),
use a different band, and hold out only 13 subjects once. Asserting a mechanism
here would be the sixth retraction.

**One quotation used**, attributed, 9 words: their stated rationale for
windowing, *"overfitting would occur when performing classification based on
subjects."* It is the field's own account of why the convention persists, and
citing it is fairer than paraphrase.

### B3 in detail — confirmed, but two clauses retracted

`20260813-220728_bandcheck`, 3298 s. At λ=0.02 applied as decoupled weight decay:

| | epoch-random | subject-wise | Δp |
|---|---|---|---|
| λ=1e-4 (used throughout) | 0.947 | 0.856 | +0.091 |
| **λ=0.02 (as published)** | **0.514** | **0.548** | **−0.034** |

Both arms sit at chance. The claim holds. But the old wording asserted two
things this run does **not** establish, and both are now cut from the draft:

1. ~~"collapses to a **constant output**"~~ — accuracy at chance is consistent
   with a constant output but does not demonstrate it; a model emitting noise
   would score the same. The logit-variance measurement came from the
   unrecorded container run. Now: *"reduces our reimplementation to near
   chance,"* with the four accuracies quoted.
2. ~~"at **every learning rate** tested"~~ — this run used the default
   lr=1e-3 only. No learning-rate sweep has a preserved record. Clause deleted
   outright rather than re-earned; it was never load-bearing.

The negative sign is not a finding. A chance-level model scores whatever
fraction the majority class happens to be in each test set, and subject-wise
folds have lumpier class balance than random ones, so a small negative Δp is
expected noise.

### The run also exposed a real limitation of our own protocol

**Δp = −0.034 would PASS V2.** The run printed `VERDICT: WELL-POSED` for a model
that had learned nothing. A degenerate model has no protocol delta, so V2 alone
cannot distinguish "no leakage" from "no model."

This is a genuine hole in the protocol as originally written, found by data
rather than by reasoning. It is now stated in §II-B: *"Δp must be read alongside
the absolute accuracies: a model that has learned nothing has no protocol delta
either."* Cheap to fix in the text, and it pre-empts a sharp reviewer — but it
would have been embarrassing to have shipped without it.

### Provenance caveat on this run

The runlog flagged `[UNCOMMITTED CHANGES]` (commit 2ced156 + the uncommitted
`--weight-decay` flag that made the run possible). The flag is now committed. A
clean 55-minute re-run would remove the only dirty record in the set; worth
doing before submission, since per-run provenance is what this paper advertises.
